I'm a Machine Learning Engineer and independent AI researcher based in Cairo, Egypt. I move between two modes of work: building production-grade ML systems, and asking harder, slower questions about why those systems behave the way they do.

On the applied side, I've built a physics-guided MRI reconstruction pipeline (fastMRI, Diffusion-GAN + unrolled ADMM), a crop recommendation system now published as an EKB journal paper, and a real-time network threat detection tool. On the research side, I've spent the last year studying the relationship between task optimization and statistical integration in neural networks — work that grew out of a genuine curiosity about Integrated Information Theory and was independently endorsed by Dr. Pedro Mediano (Imperial College London), a leading researcher in IIT and information dynamics.

What connects these threads is a preference for interpretable systems over black boxes: a Random Forest whose SHAP values tell a coherent agronomic story, an MRI pipeline whose ablation study honestly shows which component does the work, a neural network whose internal correlations can be measured and tracked epoch by epoch — not just a leaderboard number.

## What I'm interested in

- Information-theoretic views of representation learning (Total Correlation, the Information Bottleneck)
- Physics-informed / hybrid deep learning for inverse problems
- Applied ML for resource-constrained, real-world settings (agriculture, security)
- Model explainability as a design constraint, not an afterthought
